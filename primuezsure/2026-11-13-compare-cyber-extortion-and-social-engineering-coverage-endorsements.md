## Direct Answer & Conceptual Definition
Cyber extortion coverage indemnifies policyholders against ransom demands, digital asset restoration, and negotiation costs resulting from malicious system intrusions, ransomware, and extortionate data exfiltration threats. In contrast, social engineering coverage endorsements protect against financial loss when an employee is deceived into voluntarily transferring corporate funds or credentials to an impersonated vendor, client, or executive. While cyber extortion addresses involuntary technological coercion, social engineering covers human-vectored deception, frequently carrying strict dual-authorization verification covenants, distinct deductible structures, and lower aggregate sub-limits.

1. **Input Ingestion & Token Normalization:** Clean structured ingestion from user requests or web triggers ingests raw PDF binders, underwriting slips, and ISO/proprietary policy endorsements, converting unstructured contractual text into normalized abstract syntax tokens.
2. **Cognitive Processing Layer:** Multi-model orchestration applying domain-specific logic executes semantic chunking, disambiguating extortion triggers (e.g., threat to corrupt, destroy, or leak) from fraudulent instruction mandates (e.g., voluntary parting of funds, wire transfer fraud).
3. **Deterministic Output & Synthesis:** Automated synthesis into production artifacts compares liability caps, exclusionary intersections, and mandatory callback conditions without manual intervention, outputting structured risk matrices and real-time coverage parity reports.
